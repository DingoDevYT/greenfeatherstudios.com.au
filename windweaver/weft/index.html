what it was

They called it the Weft. Runic matter embedment — energy pulled out of almost nothing, out of the matter of a thing itself. You could feed it a handful of the world and it gave back light, heat, motion, more than you put in, forever, they said. Forever is the word that should have frightened us.

The planet was already running dry. So when a stone could burn without ending, it looked like mercy. It looked like the last good idea.

what they did with it

The same hands that held the coal held the Weft. They sold the sun back to us by the hour. And like the coal, like the oil, it took more than it gave once it was theirs. The ground went first. Then the water. You do not notice the tide is poison until the things that lived in it stop coming back.

what is left

No one, now. That is the honest entry. The cities are quiet in a way cities were never meant to be quiet. Only the birds stayed — they were always going to outlast us, I think, they were built for a poisoned world in a way we never were. I watch them and I wonder what they will make of all this air.

the part I cannot stop thinking about

We took it out of the ground and the ground died. Somewhere ahead of me — far ahead, I can feel it the way you feel weather — someone learns to take it out of the sky instead. They will call it something gentler. A █████. They will think, because it is the opposite direction, that it is the opposite thing. It is not the opposite thing.

history does not repeat. it embeds. // the weft again, more complex, worn like clothing //

There is a boy in it. The records I can still reach give a name and I keep reading it as mine. It is not mine. It is close enough to be cruel. ███████

— J. Ebon
